Consent

What is it?

Sexual consent is a free, voluntary and informed agreement between people to participate in a sexual activity. Consent should be active, mutual and ongoing. A person can change their mind at any time.

Consent is not created by being in a relationship, staying silent, being pressured, or assuming that someone wants to participate.

Why is it an issue?

When consent is missing, a person's boundaries and autonomy are not respected. Pressure, threats, intimidation or controlling behaviour can make a person feel unable to make a free choice.

Source: 1800RESPECT — Consent

Possible impacts

  • Physical: unwanted physical contact or injury can occur.
  • Mental: stress, anxiety and feeling unsafe may occur.
  • Emotional: someone may feel upset, angry, embarrassed, guilty or betrayed.

Choices that reduce risk

  • Communicate clearly and check that the other person wants to participate.
  • Respect a “no”, hesitation or uncertainty.
  • Never use pressure, threats or guilt to get agreement.
  • Remember that consent can be withdrawn at any time.
  • Respect other people's boundaries online as well as in person.

Sexting

What is it?

Sexting means sending a sexual message or text, with or without a photo or video. A related issue is image-based abuse, where an intimate image or video is shared or threatened to be shared without the person's consent.

For anyone under 18, sexual images involving minors can also create serious legal and safety issues.

Source: eSafety Commissioner — Sending nudes and sexting

Possible impacts

  • Physical: online situations can sometimes create real-world safety concerns.
  • Mental: anxiety and worry about who has seen or saved an image.
  • Emotional: embarrassment, shame, fear, anger or feeling betrayed.
  • An image may continue to be shared, causing ongoing distress.

Choices that reduce risk

  • Don't pressure anyone to send an intimate image.
  • Think carefully before sending anything that could be saved or shared.
  • Never forward someone else's intimate image.
  • Use strong passwords and privacy settings.
  • If someone threatens you, don't pay or send more content. Save evidence and seek help.

Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)

What are STIs?

Sexually transmitted infections are infections that can be passed between people through sexual contact. Examples include chlamydia, gonorrhoea, syphilis, HIV, genital herpes and HPV.

Some STIs can have no obvious symptoms, so a person may not know they have an infection.

Source: Australian Government Department of Health — STI campaign

Possible impacts

  • Physical: symptoms can include pain, sores, discharge or discomfort, and untreated infections can sometimes cause complications.
  • Mental: people may feel worried about testing, treatment or telling a partner.
  • Emotional: embarrassment, shame or fear of judgement can make it harder to seek help.

Choices that reduce risk

  • Use condoms and other appropriate protection correctly.
  • Get tested when appropriate and follow medical advice.
  • Communicate honestly with sexual partners about sexual health.
  • Seek reliable information from healthcare professionals.
  • Follow treatment instructions if an infection is diagnosed.
